Port-External Beamforming under Beam Management Protocols

Altough the beamforming problem has been studied extensively, much of the literature focuses on optimizing beamformers in relatively simple settings. One practical detail that deserves more attention is that 5G NR beam management protocols define beams at logical antenna ports, rather than physical antennas. Logical beamforming and port-external beamforming can therefore coexist without explicit coordination, raising a number of nontrivial signal processing porblems. We hope these problems will receive more attention in the near future, and we view machine learning as a critical tool for addressing them.

Electromagnetically Reconfigurable Antennas and Tri-Hybrid MIMO

We advocate using electromagnetically reconfigurable antennas to provide MIMO systems with an additional layer of control. One of the major motivations is to use electromagnetic tuning, for example with PIN diodes or varactors, to take over some of the beam control traditionally handled by RF components such as phase shifters. Electromagnetic tuning often consumes less power, making it particularly appealing for large-scale MIMO. Such electromagnetic reconfigurability can be realized through various reconfigurable antenna technologies. Combining such electromagnetic control with a conventional hybrid digital-analog MIMO architecture naturally leads to tri-hybrid MIMO. We find this architecture particularly interesting, as it gives rise to many new signal processing challenges and opportunities.

Radio Localization with RIS and Satellite Integration

The substantial research interest in reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) and satellite systems (particularly low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellites) in recent years, has opened up new opportunities for radio localization. RISs can serve as passive positioning anchors, providing additional geometric information to facilitate localization. Meanwhile, LEO satellites are capable of providing both communication and positioning services, although integrating these two functions within a unified satellite system remains challenging. By incorporating RISs and LEO satellites into existing cellular localization systems, we envision opportunities to enahnce positioning coverage and robustness. These possibilities have motivated our research in this direction in the past few years. Notably, beyond theoretical investigations, we have also conducted experimental studies to better understand mutual coupling effects in RISs.
